Skill or Luck? Reading a player's numbers the right way

MLB Statcast, 2023–2026 regular seasons · every plate appearance, adjusted for park and opponent

The idea

Every stat line is skill + context + luck. Context is the park and the quality of the opponents. Luck is which balls found holes. Our model estimates all three at once and keeps only the skill. The smaller the sample, the more of a hot or cold start is luck, so the more we pull the number back toward average.

How long before you can trust a stat?

The number is the plate appearances (batters faced, for pitchers) at which a stat is half skill, half luck. Lower means it settles faster.

Rule of thumb: Skill = average + (share that is skill) × (player − average), with share = PA ÷ (PA + the number in the table).
Example: Player 100517 hit .449 wOBA in 187 PA in 2026. Average is .313. Only about 48% of his edge is likely skill, so his estimate is .379.

Did it work? Tested on the next season

We made estimates from one season and checked them against what players did the following year (2023→2024 through 2025→2026).

  • Hitters' wOBA: 15% less error than using last year's raw number.
  • Pitchers' wOBA allowed: 20% less error.
  • On small samples (50–149 PA): hitters 29%, pitchers 30%.
  • Strikeout rate: 6% (hitters), 9% (pitchers), smaller because K% is mostly skill to begin with.

For hitters, this year's contact-quality (xwOBA) skill predicted next year's wOBA better than this year's wOBA skill did.

How to use this

  • Strikeouts settle fastest: about 65 PA for hitters and 69 batters faced for pitchers to be half skill. React to K% early.
  • Don't trust BABIP, especially for pitchers: even a full season of hits on balls in play is mostly luck and defense.
  • Prefer contact quality (xwOBA) to results (wOBA) for short stretches: it is half skill after ~107 PA versus ~194 for wOBA.
  • Always ask "how many PA?" A hitter's wOBA is not even half skill until about 194 PA; for pitchers it takes about 430.

Method: linear mixed model per stat and season with crossed random effects for hitter, pitcher, and park, plus a same-handed matchup adjustment; variances by REML; next-season test uses players with ≥50 trials in season one and ≥100 in season two, error weighted by season-two playing time. Defense, catcher framing, and aging are not modeled.
